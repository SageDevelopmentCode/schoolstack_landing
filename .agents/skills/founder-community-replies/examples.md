# Examples

## Good: homeschool mom planning an all-girls academy

She is early, overwhelmed, college educator (not children), business side, start with her 4-year-old daughter while homeschooling sons (15 and 13), vision of classical studies, faith and personal development, home and life management, and culture. She offered systems, operations, and data security help.

```
Hi! Building the academy you want your daughter in, while your boys finish at home, is a really steady place to start.

I founded Sage Field in Round Rock (https://sagefield.co/) from the business side, with teachers running the classroom. Zero to 40 students in about three months. I built MudKitchen to actually run it. Happy to help you get yours going, and your systems background is a gift for this stage.
```

Why it works: it mirrors her daughter, her sons, and the business-and-teachers split, then uses the school, the growth, and MudKitchen as proof. One help sentence. No feature list.

## Bad: too long

The four-paragraph version recites her whole vision, lists finances, marketing, tuition, and the parent application, explains why MudKitchen exists, and stacks three offers. Too much for a comment.

```
Hi! So glad you introduced yourself.

Starting with your daughter, while you keep homeschooling your sons through graduation, is a really steady way to build the school you want her to attend. An all-girls academy with classical studies, faith and personal development, home and life management, and culture is a clear picture. You can grow into it one family at a time.

I founded Sage Field, a microschool in Round Rock, Texas (https://sagefield.co/). I was on the business side too. I handled finances, marketing, tuition, and the parent application, and I supported the teachers who ran the academics. We went from zero students to 40 in about three months. I also built MudKitchen, the software we use to run the school, because the application, tuition, and talking with parents got heavy fast.

Your background in systems, operations, and data security is a real gift at this stage. So much of the early work is how families apply, how money moves, and how you keep their information safe.

I'd love to help you get the school started. Happy to talk through a first year that begins with your daughter, or how to set up tuition and the parent application before you bring teachers on. If you want to swap notes on systems, I'm an open book too.
```

## Bad: pitchy, generic, em dash

```
Welcome to the group! I built MudKitchen — the all-in-one platform for microschools — and we'd love to show you a demo. We help founders with enrollment, tuition, and parent communication so you can focus on what matters. Book a demo at trymudkitchen.com and check out Sage Field while you're at it!
```

Why it fails: it ignores her family and vision, leads with the product, uses an em dash, and asks her to book a demo.
