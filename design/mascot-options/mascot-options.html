Option A

Pip the mud-pie sprout

A warm little dollop of garden mud with a seedling on top that grows as your school does. Pip is the eager helper: first to cheer, a bit impatient, always sprouting a new idea.

Signature
The two-leaf sprout. The leaves perk up, droop and flop with the mood, so Pip still reads at 24px.
Voice
Bouncy, upbeat, first to say "you did it!"
Why it fits
Mud is in the name. The most ownable and the simplest shape to animate.
Watch out
A plain brown blob can drift toward a poop emoji. Keep the sprout and the clay orange at every size.

Option B

Nutmeg the acorn kid

A small acorn with a big future and a cap that never sits straight. Nutmeg is the curious explorer: pockets full of treasures, always asking "what's this?"

Signature
The tilting cap. It slides, tips and pops off with the mood, like a kid's beanie.
Voice
Curious and earnest, a little goofy.
Why it fits
Little acorns grow into oaks, a natural match for milestones and growth.
Watch out
Acorns are a common motif, so the cap behaviour has to carry the identity.

Option C

Dig the little mole chef

A bespectacled mole in a mud-kitchen apron, wooden spoon always in paw. Dig is the hands-on teacher type: stirs up mud pies, digs into every project and is quietly proud of everyone.

Signature
Round glasses, a pink nose and a spoon. Recognisable from the face alone.
Voice
Warm, wise, gently funny.
Why it fits
The most "mud kitchen" of the four. Glasses make it feel trustworthy to parents and teachers.
Watch out
More detail than the others, so the app icon needs the face crop. Moles are less instantly cute to some people.

Option D

Terra the pot snail

A snail who carries a garden on their back: a terracotta pot that blooms as families settle in. Terra is the patient one, never rushes and always notices the small wins.

Signature
Eye-stalks and a blooming pot. The flower opens wider as progress grows.
Voice
Calm, kind, quietly delighted.
Why it fits
The terracotta pot echoes the current bowl logo. The most distinctive silhouette of the four.
Watch out
A snail reads as slow, which can clash with "set up in minutes" messaging. Eye-stalks take more work to animate.

My take

Pip is the strongest brand mark. It has the simplest silhouette, the sprout works on its own as an icon, and "mud" is literally in the name. Dig has the most personality for in-app moments and earns parents' trust fastest. If neither is quite right, a common next step is to cross the two: Pip's shape and sprout with Dig's apron and spoon.

Once you pick a direction, round 2 refines it: final proportions, a turnaround (front, side, back), more expressions, and Lottie-ready animation layers.

Vector drafts, drawn in code. Every pose shares one body, so the characters stay on-model.
