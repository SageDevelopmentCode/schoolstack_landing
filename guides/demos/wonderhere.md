WonderHere Lakeland Landing Page Redesign Prompt
Goal:
Redesign my existing landing page to look and feel like a custom marketing page for WonderHere Lakeland in Lakeland, Florida, using their language, offerings, and visual vibe so it feels like a “ready-to-use” page for their team.

1. Brand Feel + Voice
Use a tone that is:

Warm, inviting, and family-centered

Wonder-filled, playful, and hopeful

Confident but not corporate; feels like a close-knit learning community

Core positioning ideas to emphasize:

“Your one-stop shop for fun, meaningful learning”

“Wonder is for all.”

Small, relationship-based, play & project-based education on a farm property

Do not sound overly techy, academic-jargony, or salesy. Keep it conversational and mom-friendly.

2. Color & Visual Direction
Use a palette and visuals that evoke:

Nature and farm life (soft greens, warm earth tones, sky blues)

Play and creativity (accent colors that feel joyful, not neon)

Hand-drawn / childlike touches where appropriate (icons, dividers, badges)

Imagery themes for the page:

Children playing, collaborating on projects, exploring outdoors

Farm environment: animals, gardens, open fields, simple structures

Mixed ages learning “family-style”

If stock images are used, choose ones that clearly show:

Outdoor, nature-based learning

Diverse children engaged in play and projects

Small-group, warm teacher-child interactions

3. Overall Page Structure
Structure the page around WonderHere Lakeland’s key offerings.

Sections (in order):

Hero Section

Schoolhouse (Small Private School)

Homeschool Programs

Farm Programs

Summer Camps

Testimonial / Social Proof Placeholder

FAQ Preview

Final Call-to-Action

4. Section-by-Section Copy & Layout
4.1 Hero Section
Purpose: Quickly communicate who this is for and why WonderHere Lakeland is special.

Include:

Headline inspired by their language, for example:

“Fun, Meaningful Learning in Lakeland, Florida” or

“Where Wonder-Filled Learning Comes to Life in Lakeland”

Subheadline that echoes their core claim, e.g.:

“Experience play-based, project-driven learning at WonderHere Lakeland, your one-stop shop for meaningful education on our ten-acre farm.”

Brief bullet list that anchors the 4 core paths:

Small private Schoolhouse (full-time & part-time)

Homeschool Programs

Farm Programs

Summer Camps

Primary CTA button:

“Schedule a Visit” or “Request Info for Lakeland”

Secondary CTA:

“Explore Programs”

Design notes:

Large, warm photo background of kids outdoors / on a farm

Overlay gradient to keep text readable

Rounded buttons, friendly fonts

4.2 Schoolhouse Section (Lakeland Schoolhouse)
Position this as the anchor offering.

Content elements:

Section Title: “Lakeland Schoolhouse: A Small Private School for Wonder-Filled Learning”

Short paragraph covering:

Small private school

Play-based, project-driven, personalized education

Family-Style™ model: multi-age, children taught core skills plus interest-led exploration with teacher mentorship

Full-day and half-day options

3–4 supporting bullets:

“Play-based and project-driven learning”

“Personalized mentorship and family-style classrooms”

“Full-day and half-day private school options”

“Safe, nurturing community on a ten-acre farm”

CTA: “Learn More About the Schoolhouse”

Layout suggestions:

Two-column: text left, image right (multi-age group learning / teacher with kids)

Add a small badge: “Registered private school”

4.3 Homeschool Programs Section
Speak directly to homeschool families.

Content elements:

Section Title: “Lakeland Homeschool Programs”

Short paragraph highlighting:

Homeschooling done in community with others

Variety of options: ages, interests, scheduling

Options for drop-off or learning alongside your child

Mention specific program types:

Friday School

Co-Op

Homeschool Collective enrichment day

3–4 bullets that speak to pain points:

“Find your homeschool community”

“Drop-off and parent-participation options”

“Programs tailored to different ages and interests”

“Enrichment that complements what you’re already doing at home”

CTA: “Explore Homeschool Options”

Layout suggestions:

Light background color different from Schoolhouse section

Photo: parents and children learning together or small group project

4.4 Farm Programs Section
Lean into the 10-acre farm as a differentiator.

Content elements:

Section Title: “Lakeland Farm Programs”

Short paragraph:

Located on ten acres of lush farmland with animals, gardens, and room to run and play

Farm as a gathering place for learning in a safe, peaceful environment

List key programs:

Wilderness Workshop

4-H Toddler Farm

Family Farm Day

3–4 bullets:

“Hands-on learning with animals and gardens”

“Toddler-friendly farm experiences”

“Nature-based programs for all ages”

“Family events that build community”

CTA: “See Farm Programs”

Layout suggestions:

Strong farm imagery: paths, animals, kids running

Maybe icon row (leaf, animal, sun) to reinforce theme

4.5 Summer Camps Section
Make it feel like “best summer ever.”

Content elements:

Section Title: “Lakeland Summer Camps”

Short paragraph:

Invite parents to “the best summer of your kid’s life”

Thematic weeks with hands-on activities, new hobbies and skills

Mention specific experiences:

Hands-on activities and new hobbies

Weekly movie day and water day

Team competitions

Interaction with nature and farm animals

Daily free play

3–4 bullets emphasizing parent benefits:

“Safe, screen-light days on the farm”

“Theme weeks that spark curiosity”

“Camps that balance structure and free play”

“Perfect for traditional school, homeschool, and preschool-age kids”

CTA: “View Summer Camp Dates & Themes”

Layout suggestions:

Brightest color section, with fun accents

Photo: kids doing water play, crafts, or outdoor games

4.6 Testimonial / Social Proof Placeholder
Even if you do not have their testimonials, design the section for them.

Section Title: “What Lakeland Families Are Saying”

2–3 placeholder quote cards with parent names (first-name only) and “Lakeland Parent” tag

Design these to be easily editable by their team.

4.7 FAQ Preview
Short preview near the bottom.

Title: “Have Questions About Lakeland Programs?”

3–4 sample FAQ headers (without detailed answers, just placeholders):

“What ages do you serve at WonderHere Lakeland?”

“How do I decide between the Schoolhouse and a Homeschool Program?”

“What does a typical day on the farm look like?”

“Is WonderHere Lakeland a registered private school?”

CTA: “View All FAQs” or “Contact Our Lakeland Team”

4.8 Final Call-to-Action
Reinforce “Wonder is for all.”

Include:

Short line:

“Whether you’re looking for a small private school, homeschool community, farm experiences, or unforgettable summer camps, there’s a place for your family at WonderHere Lakeland.”

One strong CTA button:

“Get Lakeland Info & Next Steps”
